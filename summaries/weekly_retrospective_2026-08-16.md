## 3. Key Themes

**📉 "No September Hike" Becomes the Base Case — Until the Consumer Data Arrived**
The probability of a Fed *hike* at the Sept 16–17 FOMC collapsed from ~67% (Jul 31) to ~44% after CPI/PPI (midweek) and then to **~29% by Friday** after retail sales missed badly (Kitco: "down near 29%, from about 34% Thursday night"). The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% in July; Warsh's Fed remains in a hiking posture with half the FOMC projecting another hike this year. The market is now pricing "hold," but Friday's jump in UMich inflation expectations (4.3%) is exactly the variable that forces hikes — this is a knife's edge, and the CME pricing will swing violently with every release.

**🛒 The American Consumer Just Hit the Wall**
July retail sales **−0.6%** vs +0.1% expected — the biggest drop in over a year, the first decline in nine months; even ex-autos/gas it fell 0.2%, and the GDP-relevant control group dropped **−0.4%**. UMich sentiment fell to **51.0** (−7.6% MoM), its first decline in three months. Discretionary (XLY) is the only major sector negative YTD (−1.0%), and Tapestry (Coach) was the week's worst large-cap mover (−20.6%) after guiding FY27 below consensus. The consumer narrative has flipped from "resilient" to "rolling over" — yet the S&P 500 is at records.

**🤖 AI Infrastructure: The Only Game in Town (and It's Getting Euphoric)**
The week's winners were almost all AI-adjacent: SanDisk +35.4% (Investor Day + JPMorgan upgrade: "structural inflection in NAND demand driven by AI inference"; +544% YTD, SNXX 2x leveraged ETF traded 135M shares), Nebius +47.7% (Q2 revenue +454%, AI cloud +514%), Super Micro +28% (Q1 guide more than double estimates, $60B backlog), Micron +10.7%, Dell +8.2%, CoreWeave +16.1%. **🚢 Oil Back to ~$88 Brent as the Iran War Re-ignites Shipping Risk**

Energy (XLE +7.7% wk, +38.5% YTD) is the market's inflation hedge. This is the single biggest input to the Fed's dilemma: energy-driven CPI is why the Fed is even *considering* hiking.

## 7. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

Let's state the uncomfortable arithmetic plainly. The S&P 500 is at a record with the **Crestmont P/E at 43.8 — the most extreme cyclically-adjusted valuation in US history**, above even November 2021's 43.9, and CAPE around 41 (roughly 2.3x its long-run average). Every marginal dollar of Q2 earnings growth — real, at ~25% — is concentrated in a handful of AI-capex beneficiaries: SanDisk is up 544% YTD, Micron is a trillion-dollar company, Nebius now trades *above* the average analyst price target, and Super Micro's stock is up 28% in a week while its sell-side consensus is "Hold." The market is paying perfection prices for a profit pool (AI infrastructure) whose margins are visibly compressing — SMCI's 700bp guide-down, Cisco's margin pressure despite record orders, AMAT's flat 50.4% gross margin. Revenue is real; the *earnings* per dollar of that revenue is shrinking, and the market is paying more for it. That is the definition of a valuation gap that closes violently when growth disappoints even slightly.

What's the market pricing in that's probably wrong? **The soft landing.** Friday's data was a stagflation print, not a soft-landing print: retail sales −0.6%, sentiment 51.0, and *inflation expectations rising to 4.3%* at the same time — while the Fed is contemplating *hiking* into weakness because the inflation is energy-driven (Brent $88 and the war premium is back). Record stock prices, a collapsing consumer, a hawkish Fed, and a second energy shock: one of these four is not like the others. History says multiple compression — even to a still-rich CAPE of 30 — implies a ~30% drawdown from here. The market is also ignoring that the retail-mania phase has arrived: a 2x-leveraged single-stock ETF on a +544% YTD NAND maker trading 135 million shares in a day is not institutional accumulation; it's the late-cycle soundtrack. Gold sitting 21% below its high *during a war* tells you the marginal buyer doesn't fear escalation, and Bitcoin down 28% YTD tells you global liquidity is tighter than the equity tape suggests. When the index's own internals (XLY −1% YTD, Dow negative on the week) contradict the headline, the headline is usually the lagging indicator. The bull case rests entirely on AI capex outrunning every other force — it may, but you're no longer being paid to take that risk; you're paying for it.
