## 1️⃣ The Week in One Paragraph

The S&P 500 rose **+2.51% to 7,561.67** in a week when the 10-year Treasury yield went from 4.86% to **5.15%** (+29bp, highest since the 2007 zone), gold fell **−4.63%**, the dollar hit a 1.5-year high, and the Fed's December hike odds went from ~72% to **~89%**. If you remember one thing: **the market paid 2.5% more for the same cash flows while the rate used to discount them rose 29 basis points** — a pure multiple-expansion week with zero fundamental tailwind and a hot CPI (+0.4% m/m vs +0.3% expected, core +0.3% vs +0.2%) and a 4-year-high PPI (6.5% y/y) as the bookends. Underneath the index, the week's real event was Nvidia losing its founder: the board accepted **Jensen Huang's resignation effective Nov 11**, named CFO **Colette Kress interim CEO**, and installed 73-year-old director **Harvey Jones as Chair** (verified in the 8-K filed Nov 12). NVDA fell **−12.75% on the week to $162.85 — a new 52-week low** — while AMD **+18.93%** and AVGO **+18.16%** absorbed the money. The tape's verdict was "the demand is real, the vendor is optional." Also on Friday, 90 minutes after the PPI print, the FAA issued a **nationwide ground stop** (20:54 UTC, ~4+ hours, longest in 25 years) after a communications/router failure out of Chicago — 16,000+ flights cancelled across Friday and Saturday. Nobody priced that yet.
