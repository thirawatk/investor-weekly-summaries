## 3. Key Themes

- **The long end broke, and the Treasury blinked.** The 30-year yield reached levels unseen since 2007 Tuesday as an AI corporate-borrowing spree collided with sovereign deficit financing in the same auction windows. On Wednesday, Treasury announced it would at least double buybacks targeting longer-duration debt; yields fell sharply and stocks rallied (NYT: "Markets Rally After U.S. Treasury Eases Bond Investor Stress"). By Friday the 10Y had round-tripped back up to 4.736% — Yahoo's verdict: the intervention "has been a bust." A fiscal authority openly managing its own term premium, unsuccessfully, is now the market's core macro fact.

- **Hawkish-Fed repricing ran in reverse — partially.** September hike odds collapsed from ~65% to ~44% after soft CPI/PPI prints (CME FedWatch), and Goldman called a September hike "very unlikely." Yet Chair Kevin Warsh (in the job since May 22) remains hawkish, inflation is persistent, and the bond market refused to celebrate. Everyone now waits for Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote, Friday Aug 28.

- **Healthcare stole the week.** Moderna +131% Wednesday (best day ever, intraday +177%) after Moderna/Merck reported their mRNA-4157 + Keytruda vaccine reduced melanoma recurrence in a landmark trial; Merck jumped +10%+ and finished the week +12.3%. - **Two consumers, two markets.** Target (comps +3.8%, traffic +3.6%, raised FY guide) hit a 52-week high Wednesday; Walmart posted an EPS beat Thursday but US comps of just +2.6% triggered a −9% single-day plunge. Jim Cramer flagged the widening "gap between stock prices and reality" — strong companies being overwhelmed by macro pressure even as index valuations sit near historic extremes.

- **Crypto's political squeeze.** Trump's push of the CLARITY Act and a White House crypto summit sent Bitcoin back above $68K Thursday for the first time since June, ending the week at ~$76,244 (+21%). The short squeeze liquidated $3.3B — 2026's largest. COIN, MSTR, CRCL, HOOD all surged; Robinhood +13.2% on the week.

- **AI capex meets its funding cost.** Vertiv lost $12.3B of market value (−10.9%) in the "yield shock week," lagging the Nasdaq by 8.8 pts — the market is beginning to price AI-infrastructure names as leveraged-rate bets, not just growth stories. Susquehanna notes top-five hyperscaler 2026 capex is expected to *nearly double*, which must be financed at 5%+ long rates.

- **Housing is frozen solid.** July housing starts fell 12.4% to 1.239M (single-family −9.9%). Home Depot's beat came entirely from basket size (+2.8% ticket) against falling transactions — locked-in 3% mortgage holders renovating instead of moving is now the entire home-improvement thesis.

- **Fiscal reality check.** National debt topped $40 trillion; the SPR sits at its lowest level since 1982 just as Brent touched $91 intraday; and BofA warned several consensus equity positions could reverse together if yields, politics, or AI financing shift unexpectedly.

## 7. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is pricing a Fed pivot the bond market refuses to underwrite.** Equities sit within 2% of all-time highs, with the S&P described as "nearly never pricier," while the 30-year Treasury trades above 5.27% — a 19-year high — *after* the fiscal authority itself intervened to push it down and failed inside 48 hours. Those two prices cannot both be right about the next five years. Either inflation and the $40-trillion-debt term premium are manageable (and bonds are absurdly cheap), or they are not (and equity multiples are absurdly rich). The Treasury resorting to doubled buybacks — quasi-QE without the Fed — is not a liquidity tool, it's a confession that nobody private wants duration at these auction sizes when AI capex borrowers are queuing for the same savings pool. The market treated Wednesday's intervention as a solution; it was actually the diagnosis.

**Moderna's week is the purest narrative-over-value event of 2026.** Roughly $56B of market value materialized in three sessions on a melanoma recurrence endpoint, for a company that just lost $782M in Q2, guides to negative EPS through 2027, and whose analyst consensus — Hold, average target $91 — sits 37% *below* the Friday close. Even after the biggest PT-hike scramble in years (UBS tripling to $150), the professional sell-side cannot justify the price; the market is paying today for a commercial oncology franchise that does not yet exist, at valuations that assume the melanoma result extrapolates cleanly to other tumors. It might. But that is a venture-style bet being priced like a blue chip rerating, and the asymmetry now runs badly against new buyers.

**What's being ignored: the consumer signal inside Walmart's crash.** A −9.8% week for the world's largest retailer on *softening low-end demand* directly contradicts the earnings resilience assumed in the other 499 S&P members — and contradicts gold-at-$4,680 and Bitcoin-at-$76K simultaneously. Assets are screaming both "debasement/stagnation" and "records-high risk appetite" in the same week, which historically resolves in one direction once the rate complex forces a choice. Meanwhile the AI financing loop — hyperscalers doubling capex, funded in corporate bond windows colliding with Treasury issuance — is being valued as if funding costs were still zero. Vertiv's −11% week is the first honest repricing of that assumption. Nvidia reports Wednesday; it will be quoted as an AI-demand referendum. It is really an AI-*financing* referendum, and the second question has a much more fragile answer.
