## 1. Executive Summary

The week belonged to the bond market. A violent back-up in long-dated Treasury yields — the 10-year touching ~4.75% (highest close since January 2025) and the 30-year pushing above 5.2%, levels last seen in 2007 — repriced long-duration assets across the board, snapping three-week winning streaks on the S&P 500 (-1.4%) and Nasdaq (-2.1%) and handing the Dow (-0.9%) a second straight weekly loss. Geopolitics piled on: Washington escalated its economic campaign against Iran with tougher sanctions, stoking Strait of Hormuz supply fears and driving a hard-asset melt-up — gold surged ~6.9% to a record $4,681/oz, silver ~7% to ~$69.5, and WTI jumped 5.7% to $87. The single biggest stock story was Walmart's stunning -9.2% collapse on Thursday despite a beat-and-raise quarter, the worst earnings-day reaction in ten quarters. Friday brought relief: a Treasury buyback announcement eased the long end, the Dow rallied ~1%, the Nasdaq 100 snapped a five-day slump, and Bitcoin exploded ~24% on the week to ~$77–78K as ~$4B in crypto shorts liquidated. All of this is now a runway to next week's double-header: Nvidia earnings Wednesday and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's debut Jackson Hole keynote Friday.

## 3. Key Themes

**1. The Long-End Bond Vigilantes Returned.** The 10-year ended near 4.74% (highest close since Jan 2025; 52-week range 3.97–4.75%) and the 30-year pushed to ~5.28% — its highest since 2007. This was the week's dominant macro force, driving the equity selloff Wednesday/Thursday until the Treasury's expanded buyback announcement Friday stabilized the long end. The message from the bond market: term premium and fiscal supply, not the Fed, now set the price of long-term money.

**2. A Hawkish Repricing Under a New Fed Chair.** Kevin Warsh's Fed has held rates steady since the December 2025 cut, and the debate has flipped from "when do cuts come" to "could hikes come." CME FedWatch put September hike odds at ~15% (down from >40% a month earlier, after spiking to ~28–33% around the split-vote FOMC minutes released midweek), but December 2026 hike odds were pegged near 68% by late-week reporting. Warsh heads into his first Jackson Hole (Aug 27–29) having warned he is "not constrained by market prices."

**3. US–Iran Escalation Ignited the Hard-Asset Complex.** President Trump announced a "major economic campaign" against Iran, with Treasury Secretary Bessent pledging tougher sanctions. Markets priced Strait of Hormuz disruption risk: WTI +5.7% to $87.06, Brent +6.6% to $94.39, gold +6.9% to a record $4,680.60, silver +7.0% to ~$69.5. Notably, equities largely shrugged — a risk the Damodaran section revisits.

**4. Walmart's Beat-and-Raise Got Punished — the Expectations Trap in One Chart.** WMT beat on EPS and revenue and raised full-year sales guidance, yet fell 9.2% Thursday ($114.30 → $103.84) — its worst earnings-day reaction in ten reported quarters — on slowing US comparable sales, IRA-driven pharmacy margin concerns, and an Oppenheimer downgrade citing stretched valuation. It finished the week -10.0%. When a beat-and-raise loses 10%, positioning, not fundamentals, is setting the price.

**5. The AI Capex Reckoning Got Its First Live Case Study.** Alibaba fell 8.6% Friday (–$21B market cap) after disclosing AI capex up 75% that flipped free cash flow to a ¥45B outflow — capex outrunning incremental cloud revenue by 4.5:1 despite 45% cloud growth. The entire AI complex de-rated with yields: NVDA -4.6%, AVGO -6.2%, PANW -6.9%, META -6.8% (child-safety trial start added pressure). Next Wednesday's NVDA print (~$91B revenue guided) is the verdict hearing.

**6. Bitcoin's +24% Week Was a Fiscal-Dominance Trade.** BTC ripped from ~$63K to ~$78K intraweek after the Treasury's expanded buyback program — read by crypto as a soft yield-curve-control signal — triggered ~$4B of short liquidations. The irony: the same fiscal stress that crushed equities midweek is what hard-money assets celebrated. Altcoins diverged sharply (ETH -4.3%, XRP -11.6% Friday), confirming this was a BTC-specific macro squeeze, not a broad crypto risk-on.

**7. Defensive Rotation Got a Growth Kicker: Healthcare.** XLV +4.3% led all sectors. Eli Lilly hit record territory (+6.4% on the week) as JPMorgan ($1,400 PT) and RBC ($1,500 PT) chased Mounjaro/Zepbound momentum and Medicare expansion. UnitedHealth and Merck (+2.3% Friday) added ballast. After two brutal years, large-cap pharma is being re-rated as both a bond-proxy hedge and a growth story.

**8. Retail Breadth Was Fine Beneath the Index Damage.** Friday saw 16 S&P new 52-week highs vs. 3 lows (Thursday), dip-buyers stepped in all week (S&P never got >2% from its record), and Robinhood (+13.2%) surged on a surprise S&P 500 inclusion. The market's internals are healthier than the headline -1.4% suggests — for now.

## 7. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is pricing a Fed put that the new Fed chair has explicitly refused to write.** Equities sit less than 2% from record highs while the 30-year Treasury yields 5.28% — a level last seen in 2007. That is not a coincidence you can wave away; it is a discount-rate regime change. If you value the S&P 500 with a 5.3% risk-free anchor instead of the 4% the market's muscle memory assumes, fair value drops by double digits with zero change in cash flows. Kevin Warsh spent the week telling anyone who'd listen that he's "not constrained by market prices," and FedWatch still prices ~68% odds of a *hike* by December. Yet Friday's rally was celebrated as if the danger had passed. It hadn't — the Treasury merely announced it would buy back its own debt, and the market chose to read fiscal stress as stimulus. That's not analysis; that's coping.

**The AI story has quietly become a capex arms race that the equity market refuses to underwrite honestly.** Alibaba just showed the endgame: AI infrastructure spending growing 75%, cloud revenue growing 45%, capex outpacing incremental revenue 4.5-to-1, free cash flow negative — and this at the *Chinese champion* supposedly benefiting from cheaper domestic compute. Nvidia trades at $214.72 with 49 of 62 analysts at Strong Buy and a $304 consensus target that requires FY2028 revenue of $569B (up from $216B) to be not just achieved but *believed*. Meanwhile the market punished Walmart -10% for the crime of decelerating from excellent to good. When 10% single-day drawdowns attach to beat-and-raise quarters, the market is telling you that expectations, not earnings, are the traded commodity — and expectations are at cycle extremes.

**What's being ignored is the part that doesn't have a ticker-friendly narrative.** The US just launched an economic war against Iran with an open-ended escalation ladder, oil is at $87 and Brent at $94 with Hormuz risk barely priced into equity VIX at 15. Gold at $4,680 and silver at $69 — records — are the bond market and the hard-money crowd filing a joint dissent against fiat-era asset pricing, and equity investors are treating it as someone else's trade. The consumer story is K-shaped and fraying at the wide end (Walmart comps slowing, Target's beat flattered by a one-time tariff refund), initial claims are creeping up from revised baselines, and yet the S&P's equity risk premium against a 4.74% 10-year is close to the thinnest of the post-2008 era. None of this guarantees a decline next week — Nvidia could thread the needle and Warsh could be suitably vague. But the asymmetry has shifted: the market is paying full price for perfection while the foundations — duration, geopolitics, fiscal credibility — are being repriced in real time.
