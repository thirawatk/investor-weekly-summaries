## 3. Key Themes

### **3.1 Strait of Hormuz: War Premium Unwinding at Last**
Oil plunged over 5% on Monday after President Trump called off planned strikes on Iran and announced that the U.S. and Iran had reached a preliminary deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. By Wednesday, Iran and Oman confirmed they had agreed on the geographic coordinates for a shipping route through the critical waterway. WTI crude swung roughly 10% during the week, settling near $78, while Brent ended around $83. The deal, if finalized, would remove the war premium that has inflated oil prices since the U.S.-Iran conflict began in late February. However, Iran's draft demands include a 20% cargo-value penalty and bars U.S. and Israeli vessels — terms that make a final agreement far from certain.

### **3.2 Labor Market Cracks: -23K Payrolls Shock**
The July nonfarm payrolls report was the week's most consequential data release. Employers cut 23,000 jobs versus consensus expectations of +83,000 — the second month of outright payroll losses this year. The unemployment rate came in at 4.1% (vs. 4.2% expected), but only because labor force participation declined. The ADP report earlier in the week foreshadowed the weakness, showing private payrolls adding just 44,000 jobs — the slowest pace since January. Temp layoffs surged to 921,000. May and June payrolls were revised down by a combined 103,000. The labor market is no longer cooling gradually — it's flashing amber.

### **3.3 Fed Rate Expectations Whipsawed**
The weak jobs data triggered a dramatic repricing of Fed expectations. September hike probability collapsed from 57% pre-NFP to 43.9% post-NFP, with hold odds rising to 60.4%. However, this narrative is far from settled. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh was reported (via Financial Times) to be open to a September hike if incoming inflation data runs hot. Governor Lisa Cook publicly backed raising rates if inflation persists. The Fed held rates at 3.50%–3.75% at its July meeting with three dissenters preferring a 25bp hike. Next week's CPI data (August 12) will be the single most important data point for markets.

### **3.4 Atlassian's Blockbuster: AI Beneficiary, Not Victim**
Atlassian (TEAM) surged 32.8% on Friday after reporting its best quarter in company history. EPS of $1.87 crushed the $1.50 estimate; revenue of $1.77B beat by $110M. AI users crossed one million, and the company projected ~13% annual sales growth. BofA upgraded its view, calling Atlassian an "AI beneficiary rather than an AI victim." The stock had been down 32% YTD through Thursday — the earnings beat was a violent unwind of deeply negative positioning.

### **3.5 Doximity: AI Narrative Can Still Move Stocks 100%**
Doximity (DOCS) skyrocketed 105% on Friday — the single biggest one-day move for a mid-cap stock this year — after the company outlined an aggressive AI investment strategy on its earnings call. Revenue of $156.6M beat estimates by 3.5%, but the real catalyst was CEO Jeffrey Tangney's disclosure that Doximity's new AI search tool generates 10x its operating cost in revenue. The stock had missed EPS estimates by 4.2%, yet the AI narrative overwhelmed any fundamental concerns. This is the market in 2026: the word "AI" in an earnings call can double a stock overnight.

### **3.6 Gold Breaks $4,300: Safe-Haven Bid Intact**
Gold surged over 6% for the week, exceeding $4,300/oz and posting its largest weekly gain since January. The rally was driven by a trifecta: a weakening dollar (DXY fell below 100), soft jobs data that reduced rate-hike expectations, and ongoing geopolitical uncertainty despite the Hormuz headlines. Silver surged 10.2%. Platinum and palladium also rallied sharply. Central bank buying and de-dollarization trends continue to provide structural support.

### **3.7 SpaceX: Broken IPO, Strong Fundamentals**
Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX) reported its first quarterly earnings as a public company: Q2 revenue of $7.8B (+92% YoY), adjusted EBITDA of $3.5B (+191% YoY), and a narrowed net loss of $541M. Starlink subscribers hit 12 million. Yet the stock slipped after-hours — down more than 50% from its June 16 intraday high of $225.64. The $100 billion insider lockup expired Thursday, with 255 million shares changing hands. JP Morgan lifted its price target to $240. The disconnect between fundamentals (accelerating revenue, expanding margins) and price (broken IPO) is one of the most interesting setups in the market.

### **3.8 Bitcoin Stuck at $65K: Coldcard Hack Meets Clarity Act Delay**
Bitcoin ended the week around $65,000, stuck in its $60K–$65K range since late June. The week brought two negative catalysts: a $116 million Coldcard hardware wallet hack (the largest cold-storage exploit of 2026) and the delay of the CLARITY Act — a federal crypto market-structure bill — to September after Senate Republicans blocked a vote before the August recess. Spot Bitcoin ETFs still attracted $754 million in weekly inflows, and whales accumulated $1.2 billion in BTC, but the price refused to budge. Crypto is waiting for a catalyst.

## 7. The Hard Truth — Damodaran Lens

**The market is pricing in a Goldilocks scenario that doesn't exist.** Stocks rallied because oil fell (good for consumers), jobs weakened (good for rate cuts), and earnings surprised (good for growth). But this is a logically incoherent trifecta. If the labor market is truly cracking — and -23K payrolls with surging temp layoffs suggests it is — then earnings growth cannot sustain current multiples. The S&P 500 at 7,758 is pricing in ~12% forward earnings growth. That growth assumption requires a consumer who is employed, confident, and spending. The data says the consumer is retreating.

**The Hormuz "deal" is being priced as a done deal when it is anything but.** Iran's draft demands include a 20% cargo-value penalty and bars U.S. and Israeli vessels from the Strait. This is not a normalization — it's a capitulation dressed up as diplomacy. If the deal falls apart — and the history of U.S.-Iran negotiations suggests it might — the war premium snaps back into oil prices within hours. The market has taken the most optimistic interpretation of a deeply uncertain geopolitical situation and run with it.

**The AI trade has become a narrative black hole.** Doximity doubled in a day because management said "AI" on an earnings call. Atlassian surged 33% on good numbers, but the narrative was "AI beneficiary." Meanwhile, SanDisk — which actually manufactures the hardware AI runs on — dropped 9% because guidance was merely in-line rather than extraordinary. The market is no longer discriminating between companies that are genuinely building AI moats and companies that are merely saying the word. This ends badly for the indiscriminate buyers.
